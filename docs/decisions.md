   is kept when it has >= 3 See/Do listings (see/do templates) OR >= 120 words of prose in its See/Do sections (See,

   Signal for every place: English Wikipedia 12-month user pageviews. Labels: over the whole filter-passing pool,
   hubs excluded from the calculation, popular = the top 20% by signal (threshold = the signal of the last popular
   place), everything else hidden_gem; hubs are labelled by comparing their signal with the same threshold. `hub`
   (population >= 1M or metropolis/state-capital type, districts excluded) is a flag, never an exclusion, but hubs
   count toward the 30 destinations and at most 5 are selected. Per-state minimum 4, maximum 8, PY included, 30
   destinations. Live places get the label by comparing their signal with the stored popular threshold; the tourism
   filter is not applied to them. If the signal is unavailable the entry has

D124 (2026-10-24) Scoring protocol. Each output is scored 1 to 5 on feasibility, groundedness, freshness and personalization (eval/phase9/rubric.md, written before any scoring) from a blind, seeded, shuffled sheet that hides the system, arm, plan and critic status. Blindness is PARTIAL by nature: the full system's computed route, season and budget lines and its source markers make its format recognisable; the report says so. Groundedness of the LLM-only output is low BY CONSTRUCTION (it has no sources); that is reported as a property of the baseline, not as a finding. Freshness cannot be read from dates (0 corpus items in the 18-month window, D32; Tavily returns no publish date, D108): the rubric asks whether current-status claims (hours, prices, closures) are supported by a live item with a fetch date, and the report states that limit. Comparisons are paired by request (n = 10): per-request scores, mean differences with a 95% paired bootstrap CI and the paired sign-flip test of eval/significance.py (exact enumeration, Holm over the family); with n = 10 only large differences are detectable and nothing is called significant beyond that test. Agreement: the second scorer (Nivithasri) scores a sample of 8 to 10 CORPUS-ONLY outputs (default, not yet confirmed by the owner: live outputs contain provider snippet text and D100 forbids sharing it); reported as exact agreement, within-one agreement and quadratic-weighted kappa per dimension and pooled (own implementation, tested against hand-computed values). The owner is the only scorer of live outputs, and the report says so. If the owner does not hand-score, the only honest fallback is an LLM scorer (the judge model, different from the generator) labelled LLM-scored, not human, in every table and a new decision entry, as D95 did for round-2 labels; it is not built.
